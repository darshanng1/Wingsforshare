8 questions

Working with WingsForShare

What does WingsForShare actually do?

We are a digital solutions agency based in Bangalore. We build websites and ecommerce stores, mobile apps, custom software and SaaS platforms, and we run SEO, paid media, social and business analytics around them. Most clients start with one project and keep us for the work that follows.

How much does a project cost?

It depends on the band. Our published 2026 pricing puts a professional business website at ₹25,000–₹1,00,000, ecommerce and web applications at ₹1,00,000–₹5,00,000, and custom platforms or SaaS above that. Every engagement starts with a written scope and a fixed price, so there are no mid-project surprises.

How long does a project take?

A marketing website typically takes 2–4 weeks. Ecommerce and web apps run 4–10 weeks, mobile app MVPs 6–12 weeks, and custom platforms longer depending on modules. The timeline is agreed in writing during the roadmap stage.

Do you work with clients outside India?

Yes. We work across India, the US, the UK, Australia and the Middle East. Delivery runs on overlap hours with your team, and all communication and documentation is in English.

Who owns the code, domain and accounts?

You do. The repository, domain, hosting, analytics and third-party accounts are all created in your name, and we hand over full source code and documentation on delivery.

Do you sign NDAs?

Yes, gladly — either yours or ours. Send it with your first message and we will return it signed before any detailed discussion.

How do payments work?

Typically a deposit to start, milestone payments during the build, and the balance on delivery. Retainers are billed monthly in advance. Everything is listed in the proposal before you commit.

What do you need from us to start?

Access to your current site, hosting, analytics and Search Console if they exist, plus one decision-maker who can approve scope and content. That is usually the difference between a four-week build and a four-month one.

6 questions

Websites and ecommerce

Do you build on WordPress, Shopify or custom code?

All three, chosen for the job. WordPress when your team needs familiar editing, Shopify when the store is the business, and React or Next.js when performance, custom logic or a bespoke design system matter. We will tell you which one fits rather than defaulting to a house stack.

Is SEO included in a website build?

Yes. Semantic markup, clean URLs, metadata, sitemaps, redirects, structured data and Core Web Vitals are part of the build. Ongoing content and authority work is a separate retainer, but the technical foundation is never sold back to you as an extra.

Can you redesign our site without losing rankings?

That is the normal case. We inventory existing URLs, map every one to a destination, preserve or improve metadata, and monitor Search Console for weeks after launch. Losing traffic after a redesign is a process failure, not bad luck.

Who writes the content?

We can. Content is usually the bottleneck in a build, so we offer copywriting and editorial support as part of scope. You review and approve everything before it publishes.

Will the site work on mobile and be accessible?

Every template is designed at mobile width first and tested across breakpoints. We target WCAG AA contrast and keyboard access, with focus states, landmarks and reduced-motion support built in.

Can you maintain the site after launch?

Yes. Maintenance covers updates, backups, monitoring, security patching and a monthly allowance for small changes. Or we hand over clean documentation and your team runs it — your choice.

6 questions

SEO and growth

How long does SEO take to work?

Technical and on-page fixes can shift impressions within weeks. Competitive keyword rankings usually build over 3–6 months and compound from there. Anyone promising page one in 30 days is either targeting keywords nobody searches or not being honest.

Do you guarantee rankings?

No. Search results are decided by Google, not by agencies. We commit to a written strategy, transparent monthly reporting and measurable organic growth — and we will tell you when a keyword is not worth the investment.

Do you handle local SEO?

Yes — Google Business Profile optimisation, NAP consistency, location and service-area pages, review workflows and local citations. It is the highest-return channel for service businesses with a physical catchment.

What is the minimum retainer?

Three months. SEO needs a full cycle of fixes, content and measurement before a fair review. After that it is month to month with 30 days notice — no annual lock-in.

Do you do link building?

Yes, through digital PR, partnerships and relevant placements. We do not buy link packages, use PBNs or exchange links — those tactics create risk you inherit later.

How do you report?

A monthly report covering rankings, impressions, clicks, conversions and assisted enquiries, plus what we shipped and what is next. Dashboards stay live so you can check between reports.

6 questions

Apps, software and data

How much does a mobile app cost?

A scoped MVP generally lands between ₹2,00,000 and ₹8,00,000 in our 2026 ranges, with complex multi-role platforms higher. Cost is driven by integrations, roles and offline requirements — not screen count.

Native or cross-platform development?

Cross-platform (React Native or Flutter) for most products: one codebase, faster releases, lower maintenance. Native Swift or Kotlin when you need heavy graphics, advanced camera pipelines or deep platform APIs.

Do you build BI dashboards?

Yes — Power BI, Looker Studio, Metabase and custom React dashboards. We start with metric definitions, because a dashboard everyone argues with is worse than no dashboard at all.

How do you handle security?

Role-based access, least-privilege permissions, encrypted credentials stored in a secrets manager, audit logs on sensitive actions, and documented data retention. We do not store passwords in application databases.

Can you integrate with our ERP or CRM?

Yes. We integrate Tally, Zoho, Salesforce, custom ERPs, payment gateways and legacy databases — through APIs where they exist, and controlled sync jobs with reconciliation where they do not.

What happens after launch?

You can take the code and run it in-house, or keep us on a support SLA covering monitoring, fixes and enhancements. Either way you get documentation, a runbook and a walkthrough recording at handover.
